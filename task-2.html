module 01

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack where criminals disguise themselves as trusted entities to steal credentials, financial data, or install malware — exploiting human trust more than technical vulnerabilities.

📧
Email Phishing
Mass emails impersonating banks, services, or HR — the most common form. Uses urgency, fake login pages, and malicious attachments.
🎯
Spear Phishing
Highly targeted attacks using personal info (name, company, role) gathered from LinkedIn or social media to appear legitimate.
📱
Smishing (SMS)
Text messages with fake delivery alerts, OTP requests, or bank warnings containing malicious links. Very effective on mobile.
📞
Vishing (Voice)
Phone calls pretending to be tech support, banks, or government agencies requesting sensitive info or remote access.
🐋
Whaling
Targeted at C-suite executives. High effort, high reward — attackers may spend weeks researching targets before striking.
🌐
Pharming
Redirects users to fake websites without them clicking a link — done via DNS poisoning or malware on the victim's device.

module 03

Social Engineering Tactics

Attackers exploit psychology, not just technology. These are the six core manipulation triggers used in nearly every phishing attack.

⏱ Urgency / Time Pressure

Forces fast decisions before the brain can evaluate rationally. Attackers impose fake deadlines to bypass logical thinking and create panic.

"Your account has been compromised. You must reset your password in the next 15 minutes or lose permanent access to all your data."
⚠
Defense: Pause. Real companies give days, not minutes. Go directly to the website to verify — never via the email link.
👤 Authority Impersonation

People instinctively comply with authority figures. Attackers impersonate IT, senior management, government bodies, or well-known brands.

"This is Microsoft Support. We've detected a critical virus on your device. Please grant us remote access within the next 10 minutes to prevent data loss."
⚠
Defense: Microsoft, Google, and Apple never call you unsolicited. Hang up and call the company's official number to verify.
😨 Fear & Threats

Threatening legal consequences, public exposure, financial penalties, or arrest forces compliance through emotional override rather than logic.

"The Income Tax Department has issued a warrant for your arrest due to tax evasion. Pay ₹75,000 in Amazon gift cards within 2 hours or face immediate arrest."
⚠
Defense: No government agency demands gift cards. Threats of immediate arrest via phone/email are always scams. Report to cybercrime.gov.in.
🔍 Curiosity Bait

Triggers the natural human urge to know things — who viewed your profile, what package arrived, what file was shared with you — without conscious risk evaluation.

"Someone just shared a photo of you on Instagram. Click here to see who tagged you before it gets deleted."
⚠
Defense: Never click notification links in emails. Open the actual app directly. Hover over links to check the real destination first.
💰 Greed / False Prizes

Offers of unexpected windfall — lottery wins, free gadgets, cash rewards — make victims override skepticism in hopes of gain.

"Congratulations! You are the 1,000,000th visitor. You've won a brand new iPhone 15 Pro. Claim your prize within 10 minutes by entering your card details for shipping."
⚠
Defense: If it sounds too good to be true, it is. Legitimate prizes never require upfront payment or card details "for shipping."
📦 Scarcity & FOMO

Fear of Missing Out drives impulsive action. "Limited slots," "last chance," or "only for you" language bypasses careful deliberation.

"Only 3 slots remaining for our exclusive investment program with 300% returns guaranteed. Your invitation expires at midnight tonight."
⚠
Defense: Artificial scarcity in unsolicited messages is a manipulation tactic. Legitimate opportunities don't expire in hours.
module 04

Best Practices to Stay Safe

These habits will protect you from the vast majority of phishing attacks. Make them instinctive.

  • 01
    Verify the sender's actual domain
    Click the sender name to reveal the full email address. Look for subtle misspellings — "support@paypa1.com" vs "support@paypal.com". If in doubt, delete.
  • 02
    Never click links in emails
    Navigate to websites by typing the address directly into your browser or using saved bookmarks. Email links can redirect to convincing fakes.
  • 03
    Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
    Even if attackers steal your password, MFA blocks them. Use an authenticator app (not SMS when possible) for all critical accounts.
  • 04
    Hover before you click
    On desktop, hover over any link to preview the real URL in the browser status bar. The displayed text and actual destination are often completely different in phishing emails.
  • 05
    Use a password manager
    Password managers auto-fill credentials only on the exact registered domain. If you land on a lookalike site, the manager won't fill in — a built-in phishing detector.
  • 06
    Call to verify unusual requests
    If your "CEO" emails asking for an urgent wire transfer or gift cards, call them directly on a known phone number. Business Email Compromise (BEC) is the costliest phishing variant.
  • 07
    Keep software and OS updated
    Phishing links often exploit known browser or OS vulnerabilities. Enable automatic updates to patch security holes before attackers can exploit them.
  • 08
    Report phishing immediately
    Use the "Report Phishing" button in your email client. Notify your IT/security team within the hour. Speed of reporting limits damage to your organisation.
  • 09
    Trust your instincts — slow down
    If something feels off, pause. Phishing works by overriding your rational mind with emotion. A few extra seconds of skepticism is your most powerful defense.
🚨 If You've Already Been Phished

1. Disconnect from the internet if malware may have downloaded  |  2. Change compromised passwords immediately  |  3. Enable MFA on all accounts  |  4. Alert your IT/security team within the hour  |  5. Monitor bank and credit accounts for unusual activity  |  6. Report to cybercrime.gov.in (India) or your national cybercrime portal
